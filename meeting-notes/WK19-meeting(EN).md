## 4. Meeting Workflow

1. **Shared note setup**
   - The group first opened the shared WK19 meeting note.
   - Attendance, agenda, and candidate idea slots were prepared.

2. **Individual idea drafting**
   - Each member was asked to write down one or two candidate DApp ideas before explaining them verbally.

3. **Oral idea pitching**
   - Each member introduced their ideas and briefly explained the problem, use case, and possible blockchain relevance.

4. **Clarification and comparison**
   - The team asked follow-up questions about feasibility, encryption/privacy, anti-tampering logic, and whether the ideas fit the expected DApp scope.

5. **Round 1 voting**
   - All candidate ideas were voted on once.

6. **Shortlisting**
   - The group narrowed the options to the stronger candidates.

7. **Round 2 voting**
   - A second vote was used to choose one final topic.

8. **Refining the selected idea**
   - After selecting the Mental Health Support DApp, the team discussed:
     - the main idea,
     - the core problem,
     - the target audience,
     - likely on-chain vs off-chain boundaries,
     - and expected challenges.

9. **Mapping to report sections**
   - The selected idea was then mapped to the required report sections:
     - idea,
     - problems,
     - target audience,
     - techniques,
     - differentiating factor,
     - challenges,
     - questions for teaching staff.

10. **Task allocation**
    - Each member was assigned one owner role and one supporting role.

11. **Internal deadlines and working rules**
    - Section deadlines, single-submission handling, and after-meeting drafting expectations were clarified.

## 10. Final Agreed Direction

### Working concept
A privacy-focused mental health support DApp.

### One-sentence project direction
A system designed to protect sensitive mental-health-related records by keeping private content encrypted and off-chain, while using blockchain for proof, integrity, and controlled authorisation.

### Core problem discussed
- privacy leakage in centralised systems,
- hacker attacks or unauthorised access,
- concentration of sensitive records on one platform,
- and difficulty in enabling verification without exposing actual content.

### Target audience discussed
- **Primary users:** people seeking mental health support who prioritise privacy and fear identity leaks.
- **Secondary stakeholders:** therapists, clinics, and related support institutions.

### Early architecture direction discussed
- **Likely off-chain:** encrypted treatment records, sensitive medical or personal data, detailed files
- **Likely on-chain:** hashes, proof records, access-control logic, minimal metadata
